What power means

Power is how quickly energy is used or work is done. Lifting a box onto a shelf takes the same work whether you do it slowly or quickly, but doing it in half the time takes twice the power. The SI unit is the watt: one joule per second.

There are two everyday ways to calculate it. If you know the total energy and the time, divide. If something is being pushed or pulled along at a steady speed, multiply the force by the speed.

The formula

P = W ÷ t    W = P × t    t = W ÷ P
P = F × v    F = P ÷ v    v = P ÷ F
1 hp = 745.7 W    1 kWh = 3,600,000 J

Use joules, seconds, newtons and meters per second to get watts. 1 km/h = 1 ÷ 3.6 m/s, 1 mph = 0.44704 m/s and 1 lbf = 4.448222 N.

Example: 30 kJ of work in one minute

An electric winch does 30 kJ of work in 1 minute (the default values).

A car needs about 500 N of force to overcome air drag and rolling resistance at a steady 100 km/h. Switch the formula to force and speed to see this one.

Real cars have much bigger engines than this because they need extra power to accelerate and climb hills.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing kW and kWh. kW is power (a rate); kWh is energy (an amount). A 2 kW heater uses 2 kWh each hour.
  • Leaving time in minutes or hours. Joules per minute aren't watts. Convert time to seconds first.
  • Mixing horsepower types. Mechanical hp (745.7 W) and metric PS (735.5 W) differ by about 1.4%.
  • Using P = F × v for accelerating objects. It gives the power at that instant. If the speed is changing, the power changes too.
