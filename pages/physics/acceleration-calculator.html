What acceleration means

Acceleration is how quickly velocity changes. If a car's speed climbs from 0 to 27.8 m/s (100 km/h) over 8 seconds, its speed goes up by an average of 3.47 m/s every second. Slowing down is acceleration too, just with a negative sign.

The calculator uses the standard symbols from physics class: u for initial velocity, v for final velocity, t for time, a for acceleration and s for distance (displacement).

The formula

a = (v − u) ÷ t
v = u + a·t
u = v − a·t
t = (v − u) ÷ a
s = u·t + ½·a·t²
acceleration in g = a ÷ 9.80665

All five come from the same idea and assume the acceleration stays constant. Velocities are converted to m/s first: divide km/h by 3.6, or multiply mph by 0.44704. Standard gravity, g, is 9.80665 m/s².

Common mistakes

  • Not converting km/h or mph. 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5 is not the acceleration in m/s²; convert 100 km/h to 27.78 m/s first.
  • Mixing up u and v. Final minus initial. Swapping them flips the sign.
  • Using average speed as final speed. A car that ends at 100 km/h averaged only 50 km/h along the way if it accelerated evenly from rest.
  • Assuming constant acceleration when it isn't. Real cars pull hardest in low gears, so these formulas give an average.
