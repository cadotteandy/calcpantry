How speed, distance and time relate

Speed tells you how much distance you cover in each unit of time. Drive at 65 miles per hour for one hour and you cover 65 miles; keep going for two hours and you cover 130. That simple idea gives three versions of the same formula, one for each thing you might want to find.

The calculator converts everything to meters and seconds behind the scenes, so you can mix units freely, such as a distance in kilometers with a speed in mph.

The formula

speed = distance ÷ time
distance = speed × time
time = distance ÷ speed

A handy way to remember it is the triangle with distance on top and speed and time underneath: cover the one you want, and what's left shows whether to multiply or divide.

Common mistakes

  • Reading decimal hours as minutes. 2.5 hours is 2 hours 30 minutes, not 2 hours 50 minutes.
  • Mixing units. Kilometers divided by mph gives nonsense. Convert first, or let the calculator do it.
  • Averaging speeds directly. Driving 60 mph one way and 40 mph back does not average 50 mph. Over equal distances it's 48 mph, because you spend longer at the slower speed. Use total distance ÷ total time.
  • Forgetting stops. The result is moving time. Add breaks, fuel and traffic separately.
