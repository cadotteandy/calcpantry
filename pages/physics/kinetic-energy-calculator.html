What kinetic energy is

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it's moving. It's the work needed to get it up to speed from rest, and the work it can do (or the damage it can cause) when it's brought back to a stop. Brakes turn a car's kinetic energy into heat; a hammer turns it into driving a nail.

The formula depends on mass and on the square of speed, so speed matters much more than mass. A car at 100 km/h carries four times the energy it has at 50 km/h.

The formula

KE = ½ × m × v²
m = 2 × KE ÷ v²
v = √(2 × KE ÷ m)

Use kilograms and meters per second to get joules. Conversions: 1 km/h = 1 ÷ 3.6 m/s, 1 mph = 0.44704 m/s, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 ft·lbf = 1.355818 J, 1 kWh = 3,600,000 J.

That's the same energy as dropping the car from about 39 meters, roughly a 13-story building, which is why highway-speed crashes are so destructive.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to square the speed. ½ × 1,500 × 27.78 gives momentum-like numbers, not energy.
  • Using km/h or mph directly. Convert to m/s first or the answer will be off by a factor of 13 (km/h) or 5 (mph).
  • Using grams. Mass must be in kilograms to get joules.
  • Forgetting the ½. mv² without the half doubles the answer.
