What gravitational potential energy is

Lifting something against gravity takes work, and that work is stored as potential energy. Let the object fall and the stored energy turns into motion (kinetic energy). Hydroelectric dams, pendulum clocks and roller coasters all run on this trade between height and speed.

Near a planet's surface, gravity pulls with a nearly constant acceleration, g. On Earth this calculator uses standard gravity, g = 9.80665 m/s². The Moon (1.62 m/s²) and Mars (3.71 m/s²) are also built in.

The formula

PE = m × g × h
m = PE ÷ (g × h)
h = PE ÷ (m × g)
speed if dropped, v = √(2 × g × h)

Use kilograms and meters to get joules. Conversions: 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 ft·lbf = 1.355818 J, 1 food Calorie (kcal) = 4,184 J.

Example: climbing three floors

A 70 kg person climbs 10 meters of stairs on Earth (the default values).

StepResult
PE = 70 × 9.80665 × 106,865 J (6.865 kJ)
In foot-pounds: 6,865 ÷ 1.3558185,063 ft·lbf
In food Calories: 6,865 ÷ 4,1841.641 kcal
Speed if dropped: √(2 × 9.80665 × 10)14 m/s (50.42 km/h)

On the Moon the same climb stores only 70 × 1.62 × 10 = 1,134 J.

Common mistakes

  • Using weight instead of mass. If you know the weight in newtons, PE is simply weight × height; don't multiply by g again.
  • Mixing feet and meters. With heights in feet, convert to meters (× 0.3048) before using g = 9.80665 m/s².
  • Measuring height along a slope. Only vertical height counts. A 100 m ramp that rises 5 m adds the same PE as a 5 m ladder.
  • Expecting the food calories to match exercise. Your body uses several times this much energy, because muscles waste most of it as heat.
