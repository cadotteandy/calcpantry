What Newton's second law says

A force changes how an object moves. Newton's second law puts a number on it: the net force equals the mass times the acceleration it produces. A heavier object needs more force to speed up at the same rate, which is why a loaded truck accelerates more slowly than an empty one with the same engine.

Weight is a special case. Gravity accelerates every falling object at about 9.81 m/s², so the force of gravity on a mass, its weight, is m × g. This calculator uses standard gravity, g = 9.80665 m/s².

The formula

F = m × a
m = F ÷ a
a = F ÷ m
weight W = m × g, with g = 9.80665 m/s²

Use kilograms and m/s² to get newtons. Conversions: 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 lbf = 4.448222 N, 1 ft/s² = 0.3048 m/s².

Common mistakes

  • Using pounds as force and mass interchangeably. In US units, "pound" can mean mass (lb) or force (lbf). A 1 lb mass weighs 1 lbf on Earth, but F = ma only works directly in newtons with kilograms.
  • Forgetting other forces. F = ma uses the net force. Subtract friction, drag or anything pushing the other way first.
  • Using grams. 500 g × 2 m/s² is 1 N, not 1,000 N. Convert grams to kilograms first.
  • Calling weight mass. Bathroom scales show kilograms or pounds of mass, but they actually measure the force your weight puts on them.
