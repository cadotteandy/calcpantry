What density means

Density is how much mass is packed into a given volume. A kilogram of feathers and a kilogram of lead weigh the same, but the lead takes up far less space because it's much denser. Density is a property of the material, not the object, so it's a good way to identify what something is made of.

Two units are common. Scientists and engineers use kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³); chemistry labs often use grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³), which is the same as grams per milliliter. In the US, pounds per cubic foot (lb/ft³) shows up in construction and shipping.

The formula

ρ = m ÷ V
m = ρ × V
V = m ÷ ρ
1 g/cm³ = 1,000 kg/m³ = 62.43 lb/ft³

Other conversions used: 1 L = 1,000 cm³ = 0.001 m³, 1 mL = 1 cm³, 1 ft³ = 0.0283168 m³, 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L and 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Mismatched units. Grams over liters gives g/L, which is the same number as kg/m³, not g/cm³.
  • Hollow objects. The formula gives average density. A hollow aluminum can is far less dense overall than solid aluminum.
  • Temperature matters for liquids and gases. Most substances expand when warmed, so their density drops. Air changes a lot with altitude too.
  • Trapped air bubbles during water displacement make the volume look bigger and the density smaller. Tap the object to release them.
