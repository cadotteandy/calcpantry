Why cups and grams don't convert directly

A cup is a measure of volume, a gram is a measure of weight, and every ingredient packs a different weight into the same space. A cup of all-purpose flour weighs about 120 grams; a cup of granulated sugar, 198 grams; a cup of honey, 336 grams. That's why serious bakers weigh: a scale removes the guesswork of how firmly a cup was filled.

The formula

grams = cups × grams per cup (for that ingredient)
cups = grams ÷ grams per cup
ounces = grams ÷ 28.35

Example: 2 1/2 cups of all-purpose flour

These are averages. Flours, sugars, oats, cocoa, chips, butter and honey follow the widely used King Arthur Baking ingredient weight chart, which assumes dry ingredients are spooned into the cup and leveled off with a knife. Water, milk, oil and rice are typical measured weights (water is about 1 gram per milliliter). Your own cup can differ by 5 to 10% depending on how you fill it.

Tips for accurate conversions

  • Spoon and level flour. Fluff it, spoon it into the cup, and sweep off the excess. Scooping compacts it.
  • Tare as you go. Put the bowl on the scale, zero it, and add each ingredient in turn. Fewer dishes, too.
  • Trust the recipe's own weights. If a recipe lists both cups and grams, the grams are what the author actually used.
  • Sticky stuff is easier by weight. Weighing honey or peanut butter straight into the bowl beats scraping out a measuring cup.
