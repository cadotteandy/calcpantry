How pan conversion works

What matters when you switch pans is the floor area. If the new pan has more area, the same batter spreads thinner and bakes faster; if it has less, the batter sits deeper and takes longer, or overflows. Multiplying the recipe by the ratio of the two areas keeps the batter at its original depth, so it bakes in roughly the original time.

The formula

round pan area = π × (diameter ÷ 2)²
square or rectangular area = length × width
scale factor = new pan area ÷ recipe's pan area
unscaled depth = recipe's pan area ÷ new pan area

Example: 8x8 recipe in a 9x13 pan

Pan swapping tips

  • Equal-area swaps are free. A 9" round and an 8x8 square hold the same, as do a 10" round, an 11x7 and a 9x9 within a few percent.
  • Deeper means slower. Batter that ends up deeper needs more time and sometimes 25 °F less heat.
  • Glass and dark pans run hot. Many bakers lower the oven 25 °F for glass or dark metal.
