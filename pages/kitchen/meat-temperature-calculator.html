Why internal temperature matters

Color and cooking time are unreliable signs of doneness. A burger can turn brown inside before it's safe, and a properly cooked pork chop can still be a little pink. The only dependable check is a food thermometer in the thickest part of the food. This calculator looks up the safe minimum internal temperatures published by the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, along with the rest time where one applies.

The formula

safe when: internal temperature ≥ USDA minimum (then rest, if listed)
°C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9, rounded to the nearest degree

Thermometer tips

  • Pull it a little early. Large roasts keep rising 5 to 10 °F as they rest (carryover cooking), steaks a few degrees. Take them off just before your target.
  • Check more than one spot. Especially on poultry and irregular roasts.
  • Test your thermometer. In a glass of ice water it should read 32 °F (0 °C).
  • Clean the probe between readings when going from raw to cooked food.
