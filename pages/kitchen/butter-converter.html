How butter is measured

In the US, butter is sold in 1 pound boxes of four sticks, and each stick's wrapper is marked in tablespoons. That makes volume and weight easy to swap: a stick is 1/2 cup, 8 tablespoons, 4 ounces and about 113 grams. In most of Europe, the UK and elsewhere, butter comes in 250 gram blocks and recipes call for it by weight, so you'll often need to go between the two.

The formula

1 stick = 1/2 cup = 8 tbsp = 24 tsp = 4 oz
1 oz = 28.35 g → 1 stick ≈ 113.4 g · 1 tbsp ≈ 14.2 g
1 lb = 4 sticks = 2 cups ≈ 453.6 g
250 g block ≈ 2.2 sticks

European butter and 250 g blocks

A 250 gram block is a little more than two US sticks (2.2 sticks, or about 1 cup plus 1 1/2 tablespoons). European-style butter also tends to have slightly more fat: US butter must be at least 80% butterfat, while many European and European-style butters are 82% or higher. You can swap them one-for-one by weight in almost any recipe; the higher-fat kind can make flakier pastry.

Tips

  • Use the wrapper. Slice straight through the tablespoon lines printed on the paper.
  • Soft butter by volume is messy. Pressing soft butter into a measuring cup traps air. Use sticks or a scale.
  • Melted butter measures the same. 8 tablespoons of solid butter makes about 1/2 cup melted.
  • Salted vs. unsalted: swap freely, but reduce added salt by about 1/4 teaspoon per stick of salted butter.
