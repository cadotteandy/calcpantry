How rice to water ratios work

Rice cooked by the absorption method soaks up all the water in the pot, so the ratio of water to rice decides the texture: too little and the grains are hard in the center, too much and they turn soft and sticky. Different rices absorb different amounts. Brown rice, with its bran intact, needs more water and time, while aromatic jasmine and basmati are usually cooked with less water than standard long-grain rice.

The formula

water = dry rice × ratio
cooked rice ≈ dry rice × yield (about 3 for most rice)
side servings = cooked rice ÷ 3/4 cup

These are common starting points, not rules. Package directions vary by brand, and pot size, lid fit and how hard your stove simmers all change how much water boils off. If your rice comes out wet, use a little less water next time; if it's firm, add a couple of tablespoons more.

Tips for better rice

  • Keep the lid on. Lifting it lets steam escape and throws off the ratio.
  • Lowest heat. After it boils, turn the heat as low as it will go so the bottom doesn't scorch.
  • Rest before fluffing. 10 minutes off the heat, still covered, finishes the grains evenly.
  • Big batches need a wide pot. A shallow, wide layer cooks more evenly than a deep one.
  • Cool leftovers quickly and refrigerate within 2 hours.
