How the turkey calculator works

It does three things. First it sizes the bird: about 1 pound of whole turkey per guest covers the meal, since bones make up a large share of the weight, and 1 1/2 to 2 pounds leaves leftovers. Then it estimates thawing time using the USDA's rules for the refrigerator and cold-water methods. Finally it looks up the USDA's roasting time range for a turkey of that weight in a 325 °F oven.

The formula

turkey size = guests × 1 lb (meal) · 1.5 lb (leftovers) · 2 lb (plenty)
boneless breast = guests × 0.5 · 0.75 · 1 lb
fridge thawing ≈ weight ÷ 5 to weight ÷ 4 days (24 h per 4–5 lb)
cold-water thawing ≈ weight × 30 minutes
roasting time = USDA range for the weight class, at 325 °F

Turkey tips

  • Count back from dinner. Add the thaw days, plus a day of slack, and move the turkey to the fridge that morning.
  • Check three spots. 165 °F in the innermost thigh, innermost wing and thickest part of the breast.
  • Don't forget the giblets. Pull the neck and giblet bag out of both cavities before roasting.
  • Let it rest. 20 minutes before carving keeps the juices in the meat and gives you time to make gravy.
  • Bigger party? Two smaller turkeys cook faster and more evenly than one 24 pound giant.
