How kitchen volume units fit together

US recipes measure by volume in a chain of units that each double or triple the last: 3 teaspoons make a tablespoon, 2 tablespoons make a fluid ounce, 8 fluid ounces make a cup, 2 cups make a pint, 2 pints make a quart, and 4 quarts make a gallon. Metric is simpler: 1,000 milliliters in a liter. The converter links the two systems through the exact size of the US teaspoon.

The formula

1 tbsp = 3 tsp · 1 fl oz = 2 tbsp · 1 cup = 8 fl oz = 16 tbsp = 48 tsp
1 pint = 2 cups · 1 quart = 4 cups · 1 gallon = 16 cups
1 cup = 236.588 ml · 1 tsp = 4.929 ml · 1 tbsp = 14.787 ml
result = amount × (size of from-unit ÷ size of to-unit)

Measuring tips

  • Use the right cup. Liquid measuring cups have a spout and headroom; read them at eye level. Dry cups are meant to be filled and leveled.
  • Volume isn't weight. 8 fl oz of water weighs about 8 oz, but 8 fl oz of flour weighs about half that. Convert dry ingredients with a cups to grams chart.
  • Check where a recipe is from. UK and Australian recipes may use 250 ml cups, 20 ml tablespoons (Australia) or imperial pints.
  • Round sensibly. For cooking, 240 ml per cup is close enough. For baking, use the exact figure or, better, weigh.
