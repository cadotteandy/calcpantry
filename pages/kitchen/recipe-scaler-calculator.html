How recipe scaling works

Every ingredient in a recipe is a proportion of the whole. To make more or less, you multiply each amount by the same number, called the scale factor. Get the factor by dividing the servings you want by the servings the recipe makes. The scaler reads the number at the start of each line, multiplies it, and rounds the result to a fraction you can actually measure.

The formula

scale factor = servings needed ÷ servings the recipe makes
new amount = original amount × scale factor
1 1/2 = 1 + 1 ÷ 2 = 1.5

Results under 10 are shown to the nearest 1/8 or 1/3. Larger whole-number amounts, like 250 g of butter, are shown as plain numbers, and amounts you typed as decimals stay as decimals.

Tips for scaling recipes

  • Convert awkward amounts. 16 tablespoons = 1 cup and 3 teaspoons = 1 tablespoon, so 6 tbsp is easier to measure as 1/4 cup + 2 tbsp.
  • Weigh for big batches. Cup measures drift as quantities grow. Converting to grams first keeps 3x and 4x batches accurate.
  • Rethink the pan. A scaled cake needs a pan with the matching area. Our pan size converter does that math.
  • Season to taste. Salt, chili and strong spices are worth adding gradually when you scale up a lot.
  • Watch the clock, not the math. Cooking time doesn't scale with the factor. Check doneness rather than multiplying the time.
