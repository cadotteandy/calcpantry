How oven temperature conversion works

Fahrenheit and Celsius are linear scales with different starting points and step sizes, so converting is a subtract-and-multiply job. Gas marks, used on UK oven dials, are an older scale where each mark from 1 to 9 is 25 °F apart. The converter also shows the rounded figure a recipe would print, since nobody sets an oven to 176.7 °C.

The formula

°C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9
°F = °C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32
gas mark n (1–10) = 250 + 25 × n °F · gas 1/2 = 250 °F · gas 1/4 = 225 °F
convection ≈ recipe temperature − 25 °F (about 14 °C)

Tips

  • Get an oven thermometer. Many ovens run 10 to 25 °F off their dial. A cheap thermometer tells you which way yours leans.
  • Convection: lower the heat or the time. Drop 25 °F, or keep the temperature and start checking about a quarter of the way early.
  • Preheat fully. Give the oven 15 to 20 minutes, longer with a baking stone, even after the beep.
  • Watch for "fan" in UK recipes. A recipe that says 160 °C fan means about 180 °C in a conventional oven.
