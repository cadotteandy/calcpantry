How hourly pay converts to a salary

A full-time year is 2,080 paid hours: 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. Paid holidays and vacation are already inside that number, because you're paid for those hours. Unpaid time off is not, so the calculator subtracts those weeks. Overtime hours are paid at 1.5 times your rate, the federal minimum for non-exempt workers over 40 hours a week.

Going the other way, a salary is divided by the hours you work in a year to give the hourly rate that salary is worth. That's the number to compare against an hourly job offer.

The formula

paid weeks = 52 − unpaid weeks off
annual = hourly × (hours + 1.5 × overtime hours) × paid weeks
hourly = salary ÷ (hours per week × paid weeks)
monthly = annual ÷ 12 · biweekly = annual ÷ 26 · weekly = annual ÷ 52 · daily = annual ÷ 260
rough take-home = gross × (1 − flat tax %)

Every pay period is an average across the whole year. A month isn't four weeks: it's 4.33 weeks, which is why monthly pay is annual ÷ 12, not weekly × 4.

Comparing hourly and salaried offers

  • Count real hours. A $60,000 salary that needs 50 hours a week is $23.08 an hour, less than a $25 hourly job at 40 hours.
  • Price the benefits. Paid time off, health insurance and a 401(k) match can be worth 20–30% of salary. A contract or gig role without them needs a higher rate to match.
  • Check overtime rules. Hourly and non-exempt salaried workers get overtime; exempt salaried workers usually don't, however long the week.
  • Treat take-home as rough. The flat rate here is a sanity check. Around 20–25% is typical for a single filer at this income once payroll and state taxes are counted, but it varies widely.
