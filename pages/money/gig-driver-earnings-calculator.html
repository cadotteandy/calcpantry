Why app pay overstates what you earn

DoorDash, Uber, Lyft and Instacart show you gross payouts, and often pay per active hour. Three things come out before that money is really yours:

  • Unpaid time. Waiting at restaurants and between orders counts as work because you can't do anything else. This calculator uses total hours online.
  • Your car. Gas is the cost you see, but tires, oil, brakes and repairs arrive later. AAA's 2025 Your Driving Costs study puts maintenance, repairs and tires for a new car at 11.04¢ per mile, and depreciation at another 28.9¢ per mile at 15,000 miles a year.
  • Taxes. Gig drivers are independent contractors, so nothing is withheld. You owe 15.3% self-employment tax on 92.35% of profit (about 14.1%) plus income tax.

The formula

gas = miles ÷ mpg × gas price
car wear = miles × maintenance & wear per mile
tax set-aside = (payouts − miles × $0.76) × tax %
net per week = payouts − gas − car wear − tax set-aside
real hourly = net per week ÷ hours online

Tax is figured on payouts minus the IRS mileage deduction, because that deduction is what you'll actually claim. With the IRS-rate toggle on, the calculator swaps gas and wear for 76¢ per mile. That's the high-end view: it's meant to cover depreciation, insurance and registration too.

The app's $28.57 an hour is really $20.42. Using the IRS rate as the full car cost ($304 a week) drops it to $14.91, which is closer to the truth if you're wearing out a newer car you'd otherwise keep for years.

Choosing your per-mile wear cost

Older, paid-off car

10–15¢ per mile covers tires, oil, brakes and the occasional repair. Depreciation matters less once a car is worth a few thousand dollars, but high mileage still shortens its life.

Newer or financed car

Add depreciation: 25–40¢ per mile is realistic. Every mile you put on a car worth $25,000 knocks real money off its resale value.

Tips to raise your real hourly

  • Judge orders per mile, not per trip. Count the drive to the pickup. Offers under about $1 per mile rarely pay once costs are included.
  • Log every mile. At 76¢ per mile, 400 miles a week is over $15,000 a year of deductions. Use a mileage app from the moment you go online.
  • Set aside tax on every payout. Move your set-aside to savings when you cash out, and make quarterly estimated payments if you'll owe $1,000 or more.
  • Work the peaks. Fewer hours at lunch, dinner and weekend rushes often earn the same money with less waiting.
