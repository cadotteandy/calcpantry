How dilution works

When you dilute a solution, you add solvent but the amount of solute stays the same. The number of moles of solute equals concentration times volume, so the product C × V is the same before and after. That gives the dilution equation, C1V1 = C2V2.

The equation works with any concentration unit as long as both sides match, and any volume unit as long as both sides match. This calculator converts units for you, so you can mix mM with M or mL with L.

The formula

C₁ × V₁ = C₂ × V₂
V₁ = C₂V₂ ÷ C₁   V₂ = C₁V₁ ÷ C₂
C₁ = C₂V₂ ÷ V₁   C₂ = C₁V₁ ÷ V₂
solvent to add = V₂ − V₁   dilution factor = C₁ ÷ C₂

Example: 500 mL of 0.1 M from a 1 M stock

Serial dilutions

For very large dilution factors, such as 1,000× or more, it's more accurate to dilute in steps. Three 10× dilutions in a row give 1,000× overall, and each step uses volumes big enough to measure precisely. Use this calculator on each step, with the C2 of one step becoming the C1 of the next.

Common mistakes

  • Adding V₂ of water. V₂ is the final total volume. Add V₂ − V₁ of solvent, or fill to the mark.
  • Mismatched units. If C₁ is in M and C₂ in mM, convert first. 0.1 M is 100 mM.
  • C₂ larger than C₁. You can't concentrate a solution by adding solvent. Check which number is the stock.
  • Using it for reactions. C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ is only for dilutions. For a titration or reaction, use mole ratios from the balanced equation.
