How grams, moles and particles connect

Chemists count atoms and molecules in moles because real samples contain unimaginably many of them. One mole is 6.02214076 × 10²³ particles, a number called Avogadro's number (NA). Molar mass tells you how many grams one mole weighs, so it converts between the mass you weigh out and the number of moles. Avogadro's number converts between moles and individual particles.

A handy way to picture it is a line with moles in the middle: grams on one side, particles on the other. You always pass through moles to go from grams to particles.

The formula

moles = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol)
mass (g) = moles × molar mass
particles = moles × 6.02214076 × 10²³
atoms = particles × atoms per formula

Example: 25 g of water

Water, H2O, has a molar mass of 2 × 1.008 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol and 3 atoms per molecule.

StepResult
Moles: 25 g ÷ 18.015 g/mol1.388 mol
Molecules: 1.388 mol × 6.022 × 10²³8.357 × 10²³
Atoms: 8.357 × 10²³ × 32.507 × 10²⁴

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check your units cancel. g ÷ (g/mol) = mol. If the units don't cancel, you multiplied when you should have divided.
  • Convert mg and kg first. Molar mass is in grams per mole, so 250 mg must become 0.250 g before dividing.
  • Molecules are not atoms. One mole of O2 has 6.022 × 10²³ molecules but twice as many oxygen atoms.
  • Typing big numbers. Enter 6.022 × 10²³ as 6.022e23 in the particles box.
  • Significant figures. Round your final answer to match the least precise measurement, usually the mass you weighed.
