How Nether portal coordinates work

The Nether is a compressed copy of the Overworld. Every block you walk in the Nether covers eight blocks in the Overworld along X and Z. That's what makes Nether travel fast, and it's also why portals sometimes link somewhere unexpected: the game converts your position, then looks for the nearest portal to that converted spot.

If you build the second portal at the exact converted coordinates yourself, the two link reliably in both directions. This calculator gives you those coordinates.

The formula

Overworld → Nether: X ÷ 8, Z ÷ 8, Y unchanged
Nether → Overworld: X × 8, Z × 8, Y unchanged

Results are rounded down to whole blocks, the same way the game numbers blocks (so −80.5 becomes −81).

Tips for clean portal links

  • Build both ends yourself. Letting the game generate the return portal is what causes most mislinks, because it may place it far from where you'd like.
  • Keep portals spread out. In the Overworld, portals closer than about 128 blocks (on Java) can grab each other's links. Space bases far apart or match their Nether portals carefully.
  • Make a Nether hub. Many players build a tunnel network at the Nether roof level or around Y 15 with one portal per base, so every trip is a short walk.
  • Mind the height. If two portals are close horizontally, the game picks the one nearest in 3D, so a big height difference can steer the link.
