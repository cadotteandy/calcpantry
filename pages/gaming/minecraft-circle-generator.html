How block circles are drawn

A circle in blocks is an approximation. The generator imagines a perfect circle (or ellipse) over a grid of blocks and fills each block whose center falls inside the shape. For outlines, it keeps only the blocks on the edge: those with an empty neighbour.

The thick outline counts diagonal neighbours too, so every block touches the next along a full side. That gives a wall with no gaps, ideal for towers, ponds and arenas. The thin outline only counts side neighbours, giving a lighter line that joins at the corners.

The formula

a block at column x, row y is inside when
((x + 0.5 − W/2) ÷ (W/2))² + ((y + 0.5 − H/2) ÷ (H/2))² ≤ 1

For a circle, W and H are both the diameter. Using the block's center (the + 0.5) keeps the shape symmetric on all four sides.

Building tips

  • Mark the center first. Place a marker block, then build the four straight runs at the top, bottom, left and right before filling the curved parts.
  • Build one quarter, mirror the rest. Circles are symmetric, so copying one quarter four times is faster and catches mistakes.
  • Use odd sizes for doors. A single center block lets you put a 1-wide door or window exactly in the middle of a wall.
