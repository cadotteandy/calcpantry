How sensitivity conversion works

Every game turns your view by a fixed number of degrees for each "count" your mouse reports, multiplied by your in-game sensitivity. That base angle is called the yaw. Games built on different engines use different yaw values, which is why the same number feels completely different from game to game.

To keep the same feel, you keep the same degrees turned per inch of mouse movement. That's the same as keeping the same cm/360: how far you move to spin all the way around.

The formula

new sens = old sens × (old game yaw ÷ new game yaw) × (old DPI ÷ new DPI)
cm/360 = 360 ÷ (DPI × sens × yaw) × 2.54
eDPI = DPI × sens

Tips when switching games

  • Turn off mouse acceleration in Windows (Enhance pointer precision) and in game, or no conversion will feel right.
  • Field of view matters. The same cm/360 can feel different at a different FOV because targets move across the screen at a different speed. Give it a few sessions.
  • Check raw input. Make sure raw input is on in games that offer it, so Windows settings don't interfere.
