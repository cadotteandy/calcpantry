Why wallpaper is counted in strips

Dividing wall area by the square footage on the label almost always comes up short. Wallpaper is hung in vertical strips, each cut a little longer than the wall and matched to the pattern, and the leftover at the end of each roll is usually too short for another full strip. The strip method used here, the same one paperhangers use, counts how many full-height strips you need and how many you can cut from each roll.

  1. Cut length. Wall height plus a trim allowance (4 in is standard: 2 in at the ceiling, 2 in at the baseboard), rounded up to a whole number of pattern repeats so every strip starts at the same point in the design.
  2. Strips per roll. Roll length divided by cut length, rounded down.
  3. Strips needed. Wall width divided by roll width, rounded up. Doors remove 3 ft of width each; windows remove 1.5 ft, because the space above and below still needs short pieces.
  4. Rolls. Strips needed divided by strips per roll, rounded up.

The formula

cut length = height + trim, rounded up to a multiple of the repeat
strips per roll = roll length ÷ cut length, rounded down
strips needed = (wall width − 3 ft × doors − 1.5 ft × windows) ÷ roll width, rounded up
rolls = strips needed ÷ strips per roll, rounded up

A common shortcut is strips per roll = roll length ÷ (height + repeat). It gives the same or a slightly more cautious answer, and is a good sanity check if you don't know the exact repeat.

Tips before you order

  • Check how it's priced. Many US retailers quote a single-roll price but ship only double rolls. Enter the price for the roll length you entered.
  • Watch for half-drop matches. On a half-drop pattern every other strip starts half a repeat lower; adding half the repeat to the trim allowance covers it.
  • Buy one batch, plus one. Rolls from different print runs can differ in shade. An extra roll is cheaper than a mismatched wall.
  • Measure each wall. Add widths rather than using the room perimeter if you are skipping an accent wall or a wall of built-ins.
