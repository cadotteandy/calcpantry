How much drywall a room needs

Drywall is hung in whole sheets, so the estimate starts with surface area and ends with a sheet count. The calculator multiplies the room's perimeter by the wall height, subtracts 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window, and adds the ceiling (length × width) if you include it. It then adds your waste percentage and divides by the area of one sheet: 32 sq ft for 4 × 8, 40 for 4 × 10 and 48 for 4 × 12.

Ten percent waste suits most rectangular rooms. Use 12–15% for rooms with many openings, soffits, angled ceilings or short wall sections, where offcuts are too small to reuse.

The formula

wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height − doors × 20 − windows × 15
sheets = (wall area + ceiling area) × (1 + waste %) ÷ sheet area, rounded up
screws = (wall area × 1 + ceiling area × 1.25) × (1 + waste %)
joint compound = area × 10 gal ÷ 1,000  ·  tape = area × 370 ft ÷ 1,000

Where the finishing figures come from

  • Screws: about 32 per 4 × 8 sheet on walls with studs 16 in on center (roughly one per sq ft) and about 40 per sheet on ceilings, where spacing is tighter.
  • Joint compound: USG estimates about 10 gallons of ready-mixed all-purpose compound per 1,000 sq ft for tape plus two finish coats. A standard pail holds 4.5 gallons.
  • Tape: USG's figure for paper tape is about 370 ft per 1,000 sq ft of board. Rolls come in 250 and 500 ft.

Tips for hanging and finishing

  • Hang the ceiling first, then the walls, so the wall sheets support the ceiling edges.
  • Run sheets horizontally on walls up to about 8 ft 1 in: two 4 ft sheets span the height and you get fewer, easier seams at a comfortable working height.
  • Use longer sheets to cut butt joints. A 12 ft wall covered with 4 × 12 sheets has no end joints at all.
  • Pick the right board. 1/2 in for standard walls, 5/8 in or sag-resistant board for ceilings on 24 in framing, Type X where code calls for fire separation such as garages.
  • Buy corner bead too. Outside corners need metal or paper-faced bead, sold in 8 and 10 ft lengths.
  • Use setting compound for the first coat in damp or cold conditions; it hardens chemically and lets you recoat the same day.
