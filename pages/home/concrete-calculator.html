How much concrete you need

Concrete is ordered by the cubic yard (27 cubic feet) from a ready-mix plant, or bought in premix bags that list their yield in cubic feet. Either way, the job starts with the volume of the form.

For a slab or rectangular footing, that's length × width × thickness, with the thickness converted from inches to feet. For a round column, Sonotube or post hole, it's the area of the circle (π × radius²) times the depth. The calculator then adds your waste allowance, 10% by default, to cover uneven subgrade, form bulge and spillage.

Bag yields used

The bag counts use the yields printed on standard concrete mix from Quikrete and Sakrete:

Bag sizeYieldBags per cubic yard
80 lb0.60 cu ft45
60 lb0.45 cu ft60
40 lb0.30 cu ft90

High-strength and fast-setting mixes can yield slightly less per bag; check the label if you use one.

The formula

slab volume (cu ft) = length × width × (thickness in inches ÷ 12)
column volume (cu ft) = 3.1416 × (diameter in inches ÷ 24)² × depth × count
volume with waste = volume × (1 + waste %)
cubic yards = volume with waste ÷ 27
bags = volume with waste ÷ bag yield, rounded up

The $170 default sits in the middle of the $140–$195 per yard range most 2026 pricing guides report for standard 3,000–4,000 PSI ready-mix. Real invoices add delivery, short-load fees for small orders (often $50–$150+) and sometimes pump rental, so a 1.4-yard pour can cost close to the bagged price once fees are in.

Tips for a smooth pour

  • Measure the actual depth. A slab planned at 4 inches over a dip that's really 5 inches uses 25% more concrete. Check several spots after grading.
  • Round ready-mix orders up. Plants usually sell in quarter- or half-yard steps. Running short mid-pour is worse than paying for a little extra.
  • Plan your labor. 62 bags is almost 2.5 tons of mix to lift, mix and place before it sets. For more than about a yard, rent a mixer and line up help, or order ready-mix.
  • Don't forget the base. Slabs typically sit on 4 inches of compacted gravel. The gravel calculator can size that layer.
  • Post holes: the round option assumes the hole is filled completely. A 4×4 post takes up about 0.09 cu ft per foot of depth and a 6×6 about 0.21, so subtract that if you want an exact count.
