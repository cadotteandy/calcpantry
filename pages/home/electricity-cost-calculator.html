How electricity cost is calculated

Your utility bills you for energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kWh is 1,000 watts running for one hour, or a 100 watt TV running for ten hours. To find what an appliance costs, convert its wattage and running time to kWh, then multiply by your rate. This calculator does that for a day, then scales it by the days per month you use the appliance. The yearly figure is simply the monthly cost times 12, so for seasonal items like heaters and air conditioners, multiply the monthly cost by the number of months you actually run them instead.

The default rate is $0.18 per kWh, close to the 2026 US average residential price reported by the US Energy Information Administration (roughly 18 cents). Your own rate may be very different: under 14 cents in some states and above 40 cents in Hawaii. Divide the total on your bill by the kWh used to get an all-in rate that includes delivery charges.

The formula

kWh per day = watts × hours per day ÷ 1,000
kWh per month = kWh per day × days per month
monthly cost = kWh per month × rate per kWh
yearly cost = monthly cost × 12

Ways to cut the cost

Target the big heaters

Anything that makes heat with electricity (space heaters, dryers, water heaters, ovens) draws 1,500 watts or more. An hour of a space heater uses as much energy as 150 hours of an LED bulb, so shaving heater time saves far more than turning off lights.

Use time-of-use rates

If your utility offers a time-of-use plan, run the dryer and dishwasher and charge an EV overnight, when rates can be half the peak price. Enter the off-peak rate above to see the difference.

Kill standby power

Game consoles, TVs, cable boxes and chargers keep drawing a few watts when off. At 18 cents, every watt running around the clock costs about $1.58 a year, so a 20 watt cluster of standby devices costs over $30 a year.

Replace old fridges and bulbs

A refrigerator from the early 2000s can use twice the energy of a new ENERGY STAR model, and an LED uses about one-sixth the power of an incandescent bulb with the same light output.
