How much paint a room needs

Paint is sold by coverage, not by room. A gallon of interior wall paint covers roughly 350 to 400 square feet for one coat on smooth, primed drywall, which is the range printed on most Sherwin-Williams, Behr and Valspar cans (Benjamin Moore often claims 400 to 450). This calculator defaults to 350 sq ft so you round toward having enough rather than running short halfway around the room.

To get the paintable area, it multiplies the room's perimeter by the wall height, then subtracts 20 sq ft for each door (a standard 30–36 in × 80 in door) and 15 sq ft for each window (about 3 × 5 ft). That area is multiplied by the number of coats and divided by the coverage per gallon. The result is rounded up to whole gallons, because that's how you'll buy it.

The formula

wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height − (doors × 20) − (windows × 15)
total area = (wall area + ceiling area) × coats
gallons = total area ÷ coverage per gallon, rounded up
cost = gallons × price per gallon

Getting the number right

Adjust coverage for the surface

Coverage claims assume a smooth, sealed wall. Knockdown or orange-peel texture, bare drywall, fresh plaster and masonry all absorb more. Drop the coverage to 250–300 sq ft for those. New drywall should get a primer coat first, which typically covers 200–300 sq ft per gallon.

Plan for color changes

Going from a dark color to a light one, or painting a saturated red or yellow, often takes a third coat or a tinted primer. Choosing 3 coats above is a fair way to budget for it.

Trim and doors are separate

Baseboards, casings and doors are usually painted in semi-gloss, a different product. A quart covers trim in most average rooms.

Buy it all at once

Tinted paint can vary slightly between batches. If you need several gallons, buy them together, or mix them in a larger bucket (called boxing) so the color is consistent from wall to wall. Keep any leftover sealed for touch-ups; the note under the results tells you roughly how much you'll have.
