How much grass seed to buy

Seed labels and university extension services give seeding rates in pounds per 1,000 square feet. The rate depends mostly on seed size: Kentucky bluegrass has roughly 1 to 2 million seeds per pound, while tall fescue has only about 200,000, so fescue needs about three times the weight to put the same number of seeds on the ground. The calculator multiplies your lawn area by the rate for your grass type, divides by 1,000, and rounds up to whole bags.

Ranges follow extension recommendations such as Penn State's, and overseeding uses about half the new-lawn rate. Seed blends vary, so if your bag lists its own rate, choose "Custom rate" and enter it.

The formula

rate = new-lawn rate × (0.5 if overseeding)
seed (lb) = lawn area × rate ÷ 1,000
bags = seed ÷ bag size, rounded up
cost = bags × price per bag

Tips for a thick lawn

  • Seed-to-soil contact matters most. Loosen bare soil with a rake, or core-aerate or slit-seed an existing lawn before overseeding. Seed sitting on thatch rarely survives.
  • Split the seed in half. Spread half walking one direction and half at right angles for even coverage.
  • Keep it damp, not soaked. Water lightly once or twice a day until seedlings are about 2 in tall, then water deeper and less often. Tall fescue and ryegrass sprout in 7–14 days; bluegrass can take 3–4 weeks.
  • Buy quality seed. Check the label for weed seed (ideally under 0.5%) and "other crop" content, and pick named turf-type varieties over cheap "contractor" mixes.
  • Wait to mow and treat. Mow for the first time when grass reaches about one third above its target height, and skip most pre-emergent weed killers, which also stop grass seed from germinating.
