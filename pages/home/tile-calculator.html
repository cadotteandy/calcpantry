How the tile calculator works

Tile is priced per square foot but bought by the box, and the number of boxes depends on four things: the area, the size of each tile, the width of the grout joints and how much you lose to cuts. The calculator takes the area (length × width, minus anything you won't tile), adds a waste allowance for the layout, and divides by the space one tile really covers: the tile plus half a grout joint on each side. It then rounds up to whole tiles and whole boxes, and prices the tile you actually carry home.

Waste by layout

LayoutWasteWhy
Straight or 1/3 offset10%Cuts along two walls, breakage, doorways
Diagonal (45°)15%Triangular cuts along every wall
Herringbone20%Angled cuts all around the perimeter

The formula

area = length × width − untiled area
tiles = area × (1 + waste %) ÷ [(tile L + joint) × (tile W + joint) ÷ 144], rounded up
boxes = tiles ÷ tiles per box, rounded up
grout (lb) = area × (L + W) ÷ (L × W) × joint × thickness × 144 ÷ 1,728 × 100 × 1.1

The grout line is the standard joint-volume method used by grout manufacturers: (L + W) ÷ (L × W) is the length of joint per square inch of tile, multiplied by the joint width and depth (the tile thickness) to get cubic inches of grout. We assume mixed grout weighs about 100 lb per cubic foot and add 10% for cleanup and mixing loss. Tile dimensions, joint and thickness are all in inches.

Tips before you buy

  • Price by the square foot, count by the box. A carton of 12 × 24 tile usually holds 6 to 8 pieces (about 12 to 16 sq ft). Enter the exact count from the label.
  • Buy one lot. Shade and caliber vary between production runs. Check that every box has the same lot number and blend tiles from several boxes as you lay them.
  • Large tiles need flat floors. Tiles over 15 in on a side generally call for a flatter substrate and a larger-notch trowel, so budget for self-leveler and extra thinset. A 50 lb bag of thinset typically covers about 40 to 95 sq ft depending on trowel size.
  • Rectified tile allows thinner joints. Tile with machine-squared edges can go down to 1/16 or 1/8 in; tile with pressed, slightly irregular edges usually needs 3/16 in or more.
  • Plan the edges. Bullnose, trim pieces and transitions are sold separately, by the piece or linear foot.
