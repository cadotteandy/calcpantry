How gravel is measured and sold

You measure a gravel job by volume, but quarries and landscape yards usually sell by weight: the ton. So the calculation has two steps. First, find the volume in cubic yards from the length, width and depth. Then multiply by the material's density, in tons per cubic yard, to get the weight to order.

Density depends on the stone and how it's graded. Figures published by suppliers and calculators such as CalculatorSoup and Inch Calculator put most gravel between 1.4 and 1.7 tons per cubic yard. This calculator uses:

MaterialTons per cubic yardTypical use
Pea gravel1.4 (about 2,800 lb)Paths, play areas, patios
Crushed stone, #571.4Drainage, driveway top layer, under pavers
River rock1.45Decorative beds, dry creek beds
Crusher run / road base1.6Compacted base for driveways and patios

If your supplier lists a different weight per yard, choose "Custom density" and enter it.

The formula

cubic feet = length × width × (depth in inches ÷ 12)
cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27
tons = cubic yards × density
bags = cubic feet ÷ 0.5, rounded up

Example: a 10 × 10 ft pea gravel patio, 3 inches deep

StepResult
Volume (10 × 10 × 3 ÷ 12)25 cu ft
Cubic yards (25 ÷ 27)0.93 cu yd
Tons (0.926 × 1.4)1.3 tons

The gap between bulk and bagged pricing is the main reason to buy loose gravel for anything bigger than a small bed. The $50 per ton default reflects typical 2026 pickup prices for pea gravel and crushed stone ($25–$50 a ton at the yard, more for decorative stone); delivery commonly adds $75–$150 per load.

Choosing the right depth

  • Decorative beds and walkways: 2–3 inches over landscape fabric.
  • Patio or paver base: about 4 inches of compacted crusher run, plus 1 inch of sand or fine stone.
  • Driveways: 4–6 inches of base topped with 2–3 inches of #57 or similar stone. Calculate each layer separately, since they use different materials.

Tips for ordering

  • Round up. Uneven ground eats extra material. Add 5–10%, or 10–15% for material you compact.
  • Ask how the yard sells. Some price by the cubic yard instead of the ton. Use the cubic yards figure and the "cost per cubic yard" line to compare.
  • Check the minimum. Delivery trucks often carry 10–15 tons and many suppliers have a minimum charge, so small orders can cost as much to deliver as the stone itself.
  • Moisture matters. Wet stone weighs more, so a ton of freshly washed gravel covers slightly less.
