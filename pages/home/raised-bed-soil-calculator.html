How much soil a raised bed holds

A raised bed is a box, so the soil it holds is simply length × width × depth. The only catch is units: beds are measured in feet, depth in inches, and soil is sold by the cubic foot in bags or by the cubic yard in bulk. This calculator converts the depth to feet, multiplies by the number of beds, and adds an allowance for settling. Freshly filled soil is fluffy and full of air, and compost keeps decomposing, so a new bed typically sinks 10 to 20% in its first season. Adding 10% up front means the bed stays close to full after the first few waterings.

The total is then split by the recipe you choose, and each ingredient is rounded up to whole bags separately, because you can't buy a partial bag of vermiculite. That is why the bag count can be a little higher than the total volume divided by the bag size.

The formula

bed volume (cu ft) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12 × number of beds
soil to buy = bed volume × (1 + settling %)
cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27
each ingredient = soil to buy × its share, then ÷ bag size, rounded up

Choosing a soil mix

Mel's Mix

The Square Foot Gardening recipe is equal parts blended compost, peat moss (or coco coir) and coarse vermiculite. It is light, weed-free and holds water well, which is why it works in shallow 6 inch beds. Use compost from several sources if you can, since blends supply a wider range of nutrients. It is also the most expensive mix per cubic foot, mainly because of the vermiculite.

60/30/10

Screened topsoil, compost and a drainage material such as perlite or coarse sand. The mineral soil gives the bed weight and structure and holds nutrients longer, while compost feeds the plants. It is the most common choice for deep beds and costs much less when bought in bulk.

50/50 topsoil and compost

The simplest bulk recipe and the one many landscape yards sell premixed as garden blend. Good for large or deep beds where cost matters more than a light texture.

Buying tips

  • Bulk beats bags past about a cubic yard. A yard is 27 cu ft, or 18 bags of 1.5 cu ft. Bulk soil delivered is usually cheaper once you need two yards or more.
  • Compressed bales go further. A 3.8 cu ft bale of peat moss expands to roughly 7 to 8 cu ft when loosened, and coir bricks expand several times over. Buy by expanded volume.
  • Don't fill deep beds with bagged potting mix. It is designed for containers, costs more and compacts over time. Save it for pots.
  • Top up yearly. Add an inch or two of compost each spring to replace what settles and what the plants use.
