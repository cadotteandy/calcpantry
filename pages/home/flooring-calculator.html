How to calculate flooring

Flooring is priced per square foot but sold by the box, so there are three numbers to get right: the floor area, the extra you need for cuts and waste, and how many whole boxes that adds up to.

Start with length × width for each rectangular space. If the room is L-shaped or you're running the floor into a closet or hallway, tick "Add more areas" and enter each rectangle separately; the calculator adds them. Then it applies a waste factor based on the layout, divides by the coverage printed on the box, and rounds up.

Waste factors by pattern

LayoutWaste allowanceWhy
Straight (parallel to a wall)10%End cuts, damaged pieces, trimming around doorways
Diagonal (45°)15%Every row starts and ends with an angled cut
Herringbone / chevron20%Short pieces and angled perimeter cuts throughout

These are the allowances most manufacturers and flooring retailers recommend. Add 2–5% more for rooms with lots of corners, a fireplace hearth or angled walls, or for tile with a large repeating pattern.

The formula

floor area = length × width (+ each extra area)
area with waste = floor area × (1 + waste %)
boxes = area with waste ÷ sq ft per box, rounded up
flooring cost = boxes × sq ft per box × price per sq ft

With underlayment added, the same room needs 2 rolls of 100 sq ft (underlayment isn't cut to a pattern, so it uses the floor area without waste), adding $60 at $30 a roll for a total of $760.

The $3.50 default is typical of mid-range laminate or luxury vinyl plank. Budget laminate runs about $1–$2 per sq ft, engineered hardwood $4–$9, and solid hardwood $5–$12, before installation.

Tips before you buy

  • Check the box, not the product page. Coverage varies a lot: vinyl plank cartons often hold 18–24 sq ft, laminate 14–25, and tile is frequently sold by the individual piece or smaller cartons.
  • Buy one lot. Make sure every box has the same lot or run number so the color matches, and open several boxes at once while installing to blend any variation.
  • Keep a spare box. Leftover planks are the easiest way to repair water damage or a deep scratch years later, when the product may no longer be made.
  • Acclimate first. Laminate and wood usually need 48 hours in the room before installation.
  • Mind the pad. Many vinyl planks have attached underlayment; adding more can void the warranty. Over concrete, laminate needs a vapor barrier.
  • Budget for transitions. Thresholds, reducers and quarter-round are sold separately, usually by the linear foot or piece.
