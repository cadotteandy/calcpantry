How roofing is measured

Roofing is ordered by the square: 100 square feet of finished roof surface. Because a roof slopes, its surface is larger than the footprint of the house underneath it. This calculator starts from the footprint you can measure on the ground, adds the eave overhang on every side, and multiplies by a pitch factor that converts flat area into sloped area. The pitch factor is the length of a rafter per foot of horizontal run: √(rise² + 12²) ÷ 12. The same factor works for gable and hip roofs, because every plane of an equal-pitch roof has the same slope.

A waste allowance is then added for cut-offs and the starter course: 10% for a simple gable, 15% for a hip roof (every hip needs angled cuts on both sides) and 20% for roofs broken up by valleys and dormers. Standard 3-tab and architectural shingles come three bundles to a square.

The formula

pitch factor = √(rise² + 144) ÷ 12
roof area = (length + 2 × overhang) × (width + 2 × overhang) × pitch factor
squares = roof area × (1 + waste %) ÷ 100
bundles = squares × 3, rounded up
underlayment rolls = roof area × (1 + waste %) ÷ roll coverage, rounded up

Materials beyond the shingles

Underlayment

Synthetic underlayment usually comes in 48 in × 250 ft rolls covering 10 squares. Traditional felt covers far less: a roll of #15 felt does about 4 squares and #30 felt about 2. On pitches from 2/12 up to 4/12, shingle manufacturers require a double layer, so plan on twice the rolls.

Drip edge and ridge cap

Drip edge is metal trim along the eaves and rakes, sold in 10 ft lengths that overlap a couple of inches, so round up. Hip and ridge cap shingles are sold by the linear foot; a bundle such as GAF Seal-A-Ridge covers about 25 ft. Check your brand, since some cover only 20 ft.

Things this estimate leaves out

Starter strip shingles (roughly one bundle per 100 to 120 ft of eave and rake), ice-and-water shield along the eaves in cold climates and in valleys, step and chimney flashing, pipe boots, vents and roofing nails. If the old roof has two layers to tear off, expect the installed price per square to climb.

Measure twice

For an L-shaped or T-shaped house, split the footprint into rectangles, run each one, and add the squares. Garages and porches with their own roofs count too. A roofer's quote based on an aerial or on-roof measurement will be more precise, but this estimate tells you whether a quote is in the right range.
