How the deck calculator works

Deck boards are laid in rows across the joists, so the count depends on how many rows fit across the deck and how long each row is. The calculator divides the deck depth by the board width plus gap to get the number of rows, rounded up because the last row is ripped to fit. Rows × deck length gives the linear footage of decking. It then adds your waste allowance and divides by the board length you plan to buy.

Joists are counted across the deck length at your chosen spacing, plus one at the end. Face screws are counted at two per board at every joist crossing; hidden clips at one per crossing. At 16 in spacing with 6 in boards, that works out to about 350 screws per 100 sq ft, the figure fastener makers quote.

The formula

rows = depth × 12 ÷ (board width + gap), rounded up
linear feet = rows × length
boards = linear feet × (1 + waste %) ÷ board length, rounded up
joists = length × 12 ÷ joist spacing, rounded down, + 1
screws = rows × joists × 2  ·  hidden clips = rows × joists

Because the 16 ft deck matches the 16 ft boards, there are no butt joints and the 3 extra boards are spare for culls and the ripped last row. Wider decks that need joints usually justify the full 10–15% waste.

Choosing board length and direction

  • Match board length to the deck. Boards a little longer than the deck (for example 16 ft boards on a 15 ft 6 in deck) leave room to trim the ends straight. A deck slightly longer than a board forces joints in every row.
  • Stagger joints. Where boards must be joined, stagger the joints across at least two joists and use a doubled joist or blocking so each board end has solid support.
  • Picture-frame borders and diagonal layouts use more material. Diagonal decking typically needs 15% waste and often 12 in joist spacing.

Framing is a separate job

This calculator counts decking and joists at a chosen spacing only. It does not size joists, beams, ledgers, posts or footings, which depend on spans, loads, soil, frost depth and your local building code. Most decks need a permit, and elevated decks must be designed to code. Treat these numbers as a shopping estimate, not structural advice.
