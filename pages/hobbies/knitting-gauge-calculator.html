How knitting gauge turns into stitch counts

Gauge is the number of stitches and rows in a set width of knitted fabric, usually 4 inches or 10 cm. It depends on your yarn, your needles and your hands, so two knitters following the same pattern can get noticeably different sizes. Once you know your gauge, every measurement becomes simple arithmetic: stitches per inch times the width you want gives the cast-on, and rows per inch times the length gives the rows to knit.

Most stitch patterns only work with certain stitch counts. A pattern written as "a multiple of 4 + 2" repeats every 4 stitches and needs 2 extra to balance the edges, so the cast-on must be 4 × something + 2. The calculator rounds your exact target to the nearest valid count and shows the next count up and down so you can choose.

The formulas

stitches per inch = gauge stitches ÷ gauge width
target stitches = finished width × stitches per inch
cast on = multiple × round((target − plus) ÷ multiple) + plus
rows = round(finished length × rows per inch)
your width = pattern width × pattern gauge ÷ your gauge

Example: a 21 inch wide piece in a 4 + 2 pattern

Your blocked swatch measures 20 stitches and 28 rows over 4 inches. You want a piece 21 inches wide and 24 inches long in a rib that needs a multiple of 4 + 2 stitches.

When your gauge doesn't match the pattern

Switch to "Compare to pattern gauge" to see the consequences. A pattern's stitch counts are fixed, so if you knit them at a different gauge the whole piece scales. Say a sweater is written for 20 stitches and 28 rows per 4 inches with a 40 inch chest, and you get 22 stitches and 30 rows:

Tips for an accurate swatch

  • Swatch bigger than you think. At least 6 × 6 inches (15 × 15 cm), so you can measure the middle and ignore the edges.
  • Block it first. Wash and dry it the way you'll treat the finished item. Wool relaxes and cotton can grow.
  • Use the right stitch and method. Knit in the pattern stitch, and in the round if the project is in the round, because many knitters purl looser than they knit.
  • Count partial stitches. Half a stitch over 4 inches changes a 40 inch garment by about an inch.
