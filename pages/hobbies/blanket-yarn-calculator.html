How the yarn estimate works

A blanket is mostly flat fabric, so yarn use scales with area. The calculator multiplies the blanket's area in square inches by a typical number of yards per square inch for your yarn weight. Thinner yarn needs many more yards to cover the same space, because each stitch is smaller and there are more of them. Crochet uses more yarn than knitting for the same area, so crochet adds 30%. Then it adds your safety margin and rounds up to whole skeins.

The formula

area (sq in) = width × length
yards = area × yards per sq in (× 1.3 for crochet)
skeins = round up (yards × (1 + extra %) ÷ yards per skein)
cost = skeins × price per skein

These rates are our assumptions, anchored to published blanket estimates: worsted crochet guides commonly quote about 800–1,200 yards for a 30 × 36 inch baby blanket and 3,000–4,500 yards for a 60 × 80 inch queen, which works out to roughly 0.6–1.1 yards per square inch. The other weights are scaled by their Craft Yarn Council gauge ranges. Real projects vary with hook or needle size, stitch pattern and tension, so treat the result as a shopping estimate, not a guarantee.

Standard blanket sizes

The presets use common knit and crochet blanket sizes: 30 × 36 inches for a crib or stroller blanket, 42 × 52 for a larger baby blanket, 50 × 60 for a throw, 66 × 90 for a twin, 80 × 90 for a full, 88 × 92 for a queen and 106 × 90 for a king. Bed sizes assume the blanket drapes over the sides. For a blanket that only covers the top of the mattress, measure the mattress and choose Custom.

Tips for buying yarn

  • Buy one dye lot. Colors shift between lots. Get all the skeins, plus your extra, at once.
  • Swatch and weigh. A 6 × 6 inch swatch in your real stitch, weighed on a kitchen scale, gives grams per square inch you can scale to the full blanket.
  • Watch the stitch. Granny squares and mesh use less; moss stitch, waffle, bobbles and corner-to-corner use more.
  • Count borders. A wide border adds area. Add its width to both dimensions before estimating.
