What goes into the cost of a 3D print

The slicer tells you two numbers that drive almost everything: grams of filament and print time. Filament is usually 60–75% of the cost of a print. Electricity is small, often a few cents. The two costs hobbyists forget are printer wear (nozzles, belts, build plates and eventually the printer itself) and failed prints, which burn filament and hours without producing anything you can use or sell.

The formula

filament = grams × spool price ÷ spool weight
electricity = watts ÷ 1000 × hours × $/kWh
wear = printer cost ÷ lifetime hours × hours
cost per print = (filament + electricity + wear) ÷ (1 − failure rate)
sale price = cost per print × (1 + markup)

Dividing by (1 − failure rate) spreads the cost of failed attempts across the good prints. At 5%, you pay for about 1.05 prints for every one that comes off the bed usable.

Example: a 50 g PLA part

A 50 g part that takes 4 hours, printed with $20 per kilogram PLA on a $400 printer you expect to run for 5,000 hours, at 120 W and $0.18 per kWh with a 5% failure rate:

Where the default numbers come from

  • 120 W power draw. Measured averages for popular printers while printing PLA sit around 100 W (Prusa MK4) to 130 W (Bambu Lab P1S). Heat-up briefly draws several hundred watts, and hot-bed materials like ABS raise the average. A plug-in energy meter gives you your exact number.
  • $0.18 per kWh. The US average residential rate in 2026 is roughly 18 cents per kWh according to EIA data, but it ranges from about 13 cents to over 50 cents by state. Check your bill.
  • 5,000-hour lifetime. A conservative guess for a hobby printer before major parts or a replacement are needed. Include maintenance parts in the printer cost if you want them covered.
  • 5% failure rate. Reasonable for a tuned printer and a proven file. Raise it for new designs.

Tips for pricing prints you sell

  • Charge for your time separately. Support removal, sanding and packing often take longer than the print costs. Put an hourly rate on it.
  • Batch parts on one plate. Heat-up energy and your setup time get shared across more items.
  • Price the plate, not just the part. Purge towers and multi-color waste add grams the part itself doesn't show. Use the slicer's total for the whole plate.
  • Remember fees and shipping. Marketplace fees of 10–15% can eat a thin markup.
