How the backing, binding and batting are worked out

Every quilt sandwich needs three things beyond the top: a backing, a layer of batting and a binding to finish the edges. The backing and batting are cut larger than the top because quilting pulls the layers in slightly and because a longarm frame or your hands need something to hold. Four inches of overhang on each side is the common standard; many longarm quilters ask for 4 to 6 inches, so check before you buy.

Standard quilting cotton is 42 to 44 inches wide, but once you trim the selvages and allow for pre-wash shrinkage, 40 inches is the safe usable width. Most backings wider than that must be pieced from two or more lengths. The calculator tries both seam directions, allowing an inch for each seam, and picks whichever uses less fabric. Binding strips are cut across the width of fabric, so each strip gives about 40 inches of binding.

The formulas

backing size = (top width + 2 × overhang) × (top length + 2 × overhang)
panels = ceil((backing width − 1) ÷ (usable width − 1))   (or the same across the length)
backing yards = panels × panel length ÷ 36, rounded up to 1/8 yd
binding length = 2 × (width + length) + 12
strips = ceil(binding length ÷ 40)  ·  binding yards = strips × strip width ÷ 36

Tips for piecing and binding

  • Cut off the selvages before seaming backing panels. Selvages are woven tighter and can pucker.
  • Press backing seams open to reduce bulk, and use a slightly shorter stitch so the seam survives quilting tension.
  • Mark the top edge of a directional backing print before you send the quilt out.
  • Join binding strips on the diagonal so the joins spread their bulk. The 12 inch allowance covers the joins and mitered corners.
  • Buy a little extra if you pre-wash. Cotton can shrink a few percent, which eats into a 4 inch overhang.
