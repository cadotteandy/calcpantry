How this yardage estimate works

Fabric comes off the bolt as a long strip of fixed width, so the question is how much length your pieces use. For each piece type, the calculator works out how many fit side by side across the usable width, how many rows that takes, and how long those rows are. It adds the rows together, applies your shrinkage allowance, converts to yards and rounds up to the next 1/8 yard, the smallest cut most shops will make.

Usable width is taken as the listed width minus 1 inch, to allow for trimming the selvages. Pieces are laid with their length running along the grain (parallel to the selvage), as most patterns require. If a piece is wider than the fabric, it's turned 90° and you'll get a warning to check the grainline.

The formula

pieces across = floor(usable width ÷ piece width)
rows = ceil(quantity ÷ pieces across)
row length = piece length, rounded up to a whole pattern repeat if matching
total inches = Σ (rows × row length) × 1.10 (if extra is on)
yards = total inches ÷ 36, rounded up to the next 1/8

What this estimate doesn't do

Real cutting layouts are smarter than this. An experienced sewist nests small pieces into gaps beside big ones, while this calculator lays each piece type in its own rows. That makes the result a safe upper bound for rectangles. It's less reliable for curved pattern pieces (sleeves, collars, flared skirts), bias-cut pieces, and napped fabrics like velvet or corduroy where every piece must face the same way. For those, use the yardage chart on the pattern envelope, or lay the pattern pieces out on a measured floor space.

Tips for buying the right amount

  • Enter cut sizes, not finished sizes. Add seam allowance to every sewn edge, and hem allowance where needed.
  • Pre-wash natural fibers. Cotton and linen often shrink 3–5% on the first wash, more for loose weaves.
  • Buy it all at once. Dye lots vary, so a second trip for a few more inches may not match.
  • Compare widths by cost. Switch the width selector and watch the cost line, not just the yards.
