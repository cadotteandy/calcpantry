How cross stitch fabric size is worked out

A cross stitch chart gives the design size in stitches, not inches. How big it comes out depends on the fabric count: the number of stitches that fit in one inch. Divide the stitch count by the stitches per inch and you have the finished design size. Then add a margin on every side for hooping, handling and framing. That margin matters as much as the design itself; skimping on it is the most common reason a finished piece can't be framed properly.

The formula

stitches per inch = Aida count, or evenweave count ÷ 2 when stitched over 2
design width (in) = stitches wide ÷ stitches per inch
fabric width (in) = design width + 2 × margin
(same for height) · cm = inches × 2.54

Aida vs evenweave, and "over 2"

Aida is woven in blocks with visible holes, and its count is stitches per inch: 14 count Aida holds 14 stitches in an inch. Evenweave and linen are woven from single threads, and their count is threads per inch. Cross stitches on them are normally worked over two threads in each direction, which is called "over 2." That halves the count, so 28 count evenweave over 2 gives 14 stitches per inch, the same size as 14 count Aida. Stitching "over 1" on 28 count gives 28 stitches per inch and a design half as wide, which suits tiny motifs or very fine detail.

Choosing a margin

  • 3 inches per side is the usual default for anything that will be framed, and what most chart designers assume.
  • 2 inches is enough for ornaments, cards and small pieces finished into pillows or flat folds.
  • 4–5 inches for large pieces that will be laced or stretched over board, or if you use a large scroll frame that needs extra fabric to grip.

Tips before you cut

  • Stitch a small test if you're swapping to a different count from the chart. Heavier or lighter coverage may need more or fewer strands of floss.
  • Keep the grain straight. Cut along a single row of holes or threads so the design sits square.
  • Secure the edges with masking tape, zigzag stitching or fray check. Evenweave and linen fray much faster than Aida.
  • Find the center first. Fold the fabric in quarters and start stitching from the chart's center so the margins come out even.
