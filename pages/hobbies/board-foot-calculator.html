What a board foot is

A board foot is a unit of wood volume: 144 cubic inches, or a piece 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide and 12 inches long. Hardwood lumberyards price almost everything this way because boards come in random widths and lengths. A board foot lets them charge fairly for a 5-inch-wide board and a 9-inch-wide one without a separate price for each.

The formula

board feet = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (in) ÷ 144
or: thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12
to buy = board feet × quantity × (1 + waste %)
cost = board feet to buy × price per board foot

Example: ten 4/4 oak boards

Nominal vs actual lumber sizes

The size a board is sold as isn't the size you take home. Hardwood is sold by its rough thickness in quarter inches, then usually surfaced (planed) smooth. Softwood dimensional lumber is named by its size before drying and planing. Board feet are figured on the nominal (rough) numbers, so use those in the calculator, but plan your project around the actual dimensions.

Surfaced thicknesses vary a little between mills. If you buy rough lumber and plane it yourself, expect to lose about 3/16 to 1/4 inch of thickness on a 4/4 board.

Tips for buying hardwood

  • Buy more than your cut list. 15% is a minimum for good stock; 25–30% is safer for lower grades or many small parts.
  • Watch the grade. FAS (firsts and seconds) yields long, clear cuts. Common grades cost less but have more defects to cut around.
  • Confirm how widths are measured. Rough boards with uneven edges are measured at their usable width; some yards round to the nearest inch.
  • Check thickness for thin parts. If your project needs 1-inch finished stock, 4/4 won't do. Buy 5/4.
