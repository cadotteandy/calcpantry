How to calculate profit on a flip

The sale price is never what you make. Between the buyer's payment and your pocket sit the platform's fees, the shipping label, packing supplies and, if you're honest about it, the gas and time it took to find the item. This calculator subtracts all of them, then shows the result three ways:

  • ROI is profit divided by what you paid for the item. It shows how hard your sourcing money worked, and it's the number most resellers use to decide what to buy.
  • Margin is profit divided by the sale price: how much of each sale you keep.
  • Profit per hour divides profit by the time you spent sourcing, listing, packing and shipping. It's the number that tells you whether a flip was worth doing at all.

The max buy price works backward: given the expected sale price and costs, it's the most you can pay and still hit your target ROI. It's the number to have in your head when you're standing in a thrift store.

The formula

costs = platform fees + shipping + supplies + miles × cost per mile
profit = sale price − costs − buy price
ROI = profit ÷ buy price
margin = profit ÷ sale price
max buy price = (sale price − costs) ÷ (1 + target ROI)

Example: a $12 jacket sold for $45 on eBay

You buy a jacket at a thrift store for $12, sell it on eBay for $45 with free shipping, and pay $8 for the label and $1 for a poly mailer. Listing, packing and the post office run take half an hour, and you value your time at $20 an hour.

About the fee presets

The presets use each platform's standard US rates for 2026, applied to the sale price: eBay 13.6% + $0.40, Poshmark 20% (a flat $2.95 under $15), Mercari 10%, and Etsy's 6.5% transaction fee plus 3% + $0.25 processing plus the $0.20 listing fee. They're built for quick comparisons. eBay and Etsy also charge on shipping and some taxes, and Poshmark buyers pay the shipping label, so set "Shipping you pay" to $0 for Poshmark sales.

Tips for better flips

  • Decide your max before you shop. Look up sold listings, plug in the expected price, and let the max buy number keep you from overpaying.
  • Count every trip. A 20-mile sourcing run at 76 cents per mile costs $15.20, more than many items make.
  • Be wary of cheap items. A $3 item that sells for $12 shows a big ROI, but fees and a label can leave almost nothing per hour of work.
  • Track your real hourly rate. If it's consistently below your target, raise your minimum sale price or source in bulk.
  • Keep records for taxes. Profit from selling items for more than you paid is taxable income, and mileage, supplies and fees are deductible costs.
