How eBay fees work in 2026

eBay's main charge is the final value fee. For most categories it's 13.6% of the total sale amount, plus a per-order fee of $0.40 ($0.30 when the order total is $10 or less). You only pay it when an item sells.

The detail that surprises new sellers: "total sale amount" means everything the buyer pays. That includes the item price, the shipping you charge, and the sales tax eBay collects on the buyer's behalf. You never receive the tax (eBay sends it to the state), but you still pay the fee on it.

The formula

order total = (price + shipping charged) × (1 + sales tax rate)
final value fee = order total × 13.6%
eBay fees = final value fee + $0.40 + promoted ad fee
profit = price + shipping charged − eBay fees − item cost − your shipping cost

Example: a $50 sale

You sell a jacket for $50 with free shipping. The buyer pays 7% sales tax, so the order total is $53.50.

Five ways to keep more of each sale

  • Price with fees in mind. Use the break-even number above as your floor when accepting offers.
  • Weigh shipping before you list. Underestimating a label is the most common way flips lose money.
  • Only promote when it pays. A 5% ad rate on a $50 item costs $2.50; worth it for slow sellers, not for items that sell anyway.
  • Watch your category. Books and media carry higher rates; some categories are much lower.
  • Consider a Store if you list more than 250 items a month. It adds free listings and can lower fees in some categories.
