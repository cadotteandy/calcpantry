How Depop fees work in 2026

Depop dropped its 10% selling fee for US sellers in 2024, so a standard US sale now carries just one charge: the payment processing fee of 3.3% + $0.45 per order through Depop Payments. There are no listing fees and no monthly subscription.

The processing fee is calculated on everything the buyer pays: the item price, the shipping, and any sales tax Depop collects. You never receive the tax (Depop sends it to the state), but you still pay 3.3% on it. That's why the calculator asks for the buyer's tax rate.

The one optional fee is Boosted Listings. Boosts started on or after March 23, 2026 cost 12% of the item price, up from 8%; boosts started before that date keep the old rate. If you arrange your own shipping, the boost fee applies to the shipping you charge as well. With a Depop label, it's charged on the item price only. You pay it only when the item sells within 28 days of a buyer viewing, liking or clicking the boosted listing.

The formula

sale = item price + shipping buyer pays
processing fee = sale × (1 + sales tax rate) × 3.3% + $0.45
boosting fee = item price × 12% (plus shipping × 12% if you ship yourself)
Depop fees = processing fee + boosting fee
profit = sale − Depop fees − item cost − your shipping cost

Example: a $40 sale with $6 shipping

You sell a vintage tee for $40 and the buyer pays $6 for shipping, which covers your $6 label. You paid $10 for the shirt. There's no boost, and we'll leave sales tax out to keep the numbers clean.

Tips for keeping more of each Depop sale

  • Boost selectively. A boost that sells a stale item is worth 12%. Boosting items that would sell anyway just hands Depop an extra cut.
  • Push bundles. One order pays one $0.45 fixed fee, so three items in a bundle save you $0.90 compared with three separate sales.
  • Check the label cost before offering free shipping. When you cover shipping, it comes straight out of your profit. Enter it in "Your shipping cost" and set buyer shipping to $0.
  • Use the break-even price as your offer floor. Anything below it loses money once fees and the label are paid.
  • Remember income tax. Depop issues a 1099-K once you pass the IRS reporting threshold, and profit on items sold above what you paid is taxable.
