How RAID capacity works

RAID combines several drives into one volume. Some levels split data across drives for speed, some copy it for safety, and some store parity: extra data that lets the array rebuild a failed drive. That protection costs space, so usable capacity is always less than the total of the drives (except RAID 0, which has no protection at all).

The formulas (n drives of size s)

RAID 0: n × s
RAID 1: s
RAID 5: (n − 1) × s
RAID 6: (n − 2) × s
RAID 10: (n ÷ 2) × s
