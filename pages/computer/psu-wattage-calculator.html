How the estimate works

The two parts that matter most are the processor and the graphics card. Together they usually make up 80% or more of a gaming PC's power draw. This calculator uses each CPU's maximum package power (what it can sustain under heavy load) and each graphics card's official total board power, then adds typical figures for everything else.

It then recommends a power supply rated so your peak load sits around 70% of its capacity. That leaves headroom for brief GPU spikes, keeps the PSU in its efficient range and lets you upgrade later.

The formula

load = CPU + GPU + 50 (motherboard) + 15 (USB) + 5 × RAM sticks + 7 × SSDs + 9 × HDDs + 3 × fans (+ 15 for pump/RGB)
recommended PSU = load ÷ 0.7, rounded up to a standard size

Example: Ryzen 7 9800X3D + RTX 4070

Buying tips

  • Quality over watts. A good 750 W unit from a reputable maker beats a no-name 1000 W one. Look at independent reviews.
  • ATX 3.x for new GPUs. Current high-end NVIDIA cards use the 12V-2x6 connector; a native cable avoids bulky adapters.
  • Gaming draw is lower than peak. Games rarely max the CPU and GPU at the same moment, so typical draw is often well under the peak shown.
