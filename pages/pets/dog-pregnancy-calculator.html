How dog due dates are calculated

A dog's pregnancy lasts about 63 days from ovulation, roughly nine weeks. The complication is that breeding and ovulation don't always happen on the same day. Dog eggs need a couple of days to mature after they're released, and sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for several days, so a mating can take place days before or after the eggs are ready. That is why a due date counted from the breeding date comes with a wide window: most litters arrive 58 to 68 days after mating, and the Merck Veterinary Manual gives an outer range of 58 to 72 days from the first breeding.

Breeders who use progesterone blood tests to pinpoint ovulation get far more precise dates. Whelping is typically 63 ± 1–2 days after ovulation or 65 ± 1–2 days after the LH surge, which comes about two days before ovulation. Choose the date type that matches what you know.

The formula

from breeding date: due = breeding date + 63 days (window 58–68)
from ovulation date: due = ovulation date + 63 days (window 61–65)
from LH surge date: due = LH surge date + 65 days (window 63–67)
days along = today − breeding (or ovulation) date

The three trimesters

Weeks 1–3 (days 0–21)

Fertilized eggs travel to the uterus and implant around days 18–21. There are usually no visible changes, though some dogs have mild morning sickness or a slightly reduced appetite around week three. Keep feeding and exercise normal.

Weeks 4–6 (days 22–42)

This is the window to confirm the pregnancy with an ultrasound or relaxin test. Fetuses grow quickly, and her belly and nipples may start to enlarge. From about week five or six, begin a gradual switch to a puppy or all-life-stages food.

Weeks 7–9 (days 43–63)

Her abdomen grows noticeably and you may see puppies moving. An x-ray after day 55 gives the best puppy count, which helps you know when she's finished whelping. Introduce the whelping box a week or two ahead so she's comfortable in it, and start taking her rectal temperature twice a day. Normal is about 100–102.5°F; a drop below about 99°F usually means labor within 12–24 hours.

Practical tips

  • Book a vet check early. Confirm the pregnancy, discuss nutrition and parasite control, and ask what signs should prompt an emergency call.
  • Avoid unprescribed supplements. Extra calcium during pregnancy can increase the risk of dangerously low calcium (eclampsia) after whelping.
  • Know your emergency plan. Save the number of an emergency clinic that's open nights and weekends before the due date.
  • Watch the clock during labor. Call a vet for 30 minutes of hard straining without a puppy, more than 2–4 hours between puppies, or green-black discharge before the first puppy.
