How this dog food calculator works

Vets estimate a dog's calorie needs in two steps. First they calculate the resting energy requirement (RER), the calories a dog burns lying still in a comfortable room. Then they multiply it by a life stage factor to get the daily energy requirement, sometimes called maintenance energy requirement (MER). This is the approach used in the WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee toolkit and the AAHA nutrition guidelines.

Once you know calories, the cups are just division: calories per day divided by the kcal per cup printed on your food. That's why this calculator asks for the label value instead of assuming a "standard" cup. Dry foods range from about 300 to over 450 kcal per cup, so the same dog can need 1.8 cups of one food and 2.6 cups of another.

The formula

weight in kg = weight in lb × 0.4536
RER = 70 × (weight in kg)0.75
daily calories = RER × life stage factor
cups per day = daily calories ÷ kcal per cup
days per bag = (bag lb × 0.4536 × kcal per kg) ÷ daily calories

These are the commonly published textbook factors. Heavy working dogs such as sled dogs can need 4 to 8 times RER, which is beyond what a general calculator should guess at.

Tips for getting portions right

  • Use a real measuring cup, or better, a scale. A "cup" scooped with a coffee mug can be 30% more than a standard 8 oz cup. Weighing grams is the most accurate method.
  • Judge by body condition, not the number. You should be able to feel the ribs easily under a thin layer of fat and see a waist from above. If not, cut portions by 10% and recheck in 2–4 weeks.
  • Individual dogs vary a lot. Two dogs of the same weight can differ in needs by 20–30% or more. Treat the result as a starting point.
  • Count treats and chews. Keep them to about 10% of daily calories and trim meals to match.
  • Puppies change fast. Recalculate every couple of weeks as they grow, and use their current weight.

This calculator is an educational estimate, not veterinary advice. Check your food's label and ask your vet, especially for puppies, pregnant or nursing dogs, seniors, or dogs with a medical condition.
