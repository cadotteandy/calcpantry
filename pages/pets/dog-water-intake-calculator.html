How much water does a dog need?

The rule of thumb most vets use is about 1 fluid ounce of water per pound of body weight per day. In metric that is roughly 65 ml per kg, and published guidance for healthy adult dogs at rest runs from about 50 to 65 ml per kg. Some sources give a wider range of ½ to 1 oz per pound, because a relaxed dog in a cool house needs less than an active dog in summer.

That figure is a dog's total water need, and not all of it comes from the bowl. Dry kibble is only around 10% moisture, but canned and fresh foods are 70–80% water. A dog on an all-wet diet can get a large share of its water from meals and drink much less, so this calculator reduces the bowl amount for mixed and wet diets.

The formula

total need (fl oz) = weight in lb × 1 × activity factor × weather factor
water to drink = total need × diet factor
activity: low 0.8 · moderate 1.0 · high 1.25 · working 1.5
weather: cool 0.9 · mild 1.0 · hot 1.3   diet: dry 1.0 · mixed 0.8 · wet 0.6
cups = fl oz ÷ 8   ml = fl oz × 29.57
vet threshold = 100 ml × weight in kg × diet factor

The adjustment factors are practical estimates, not lab measurements; real dogs vary, which is why the result comes with a ±20% day-to-day range.

Warning signs to watch for

Drinking too much

Excessive thirst (polydipsia) is usually defined as more than about 100 ml per kg per day, roughly 1.5 oz per pound. Emptying the bowl far more often than usual, needing to go out at night or having accidents in a house-trained dog are common clues. Increased thirst can be an early sign of kidney disease, diabetes, Cushing's disease, liver disease, a urinary or uterine infection (pyometra in unspayed females), or a side effect of drugs such as steroids. Don't cut back the water; book a vet visit and bring a urine sample if you can.

Drinking too little

Watch for dry or tacky gums, thick saliva, sunken eyes, lethargy, dark urine and skin that is slow to spring back when lifted at the shoulders. A dog that refuses water for a day, or is also vomiting or has diarrhea, needs a vet promptly.

Practical tips

  • Measure before you worry. Pour a measured amount into the bowl each morning, log refills and measure what is left after 24 hours. Average two or three days.
  • Never restrict water for a healthy dog unless your vet asks you to. Keep fresh water available at all times.
  • Carry water on outings. On hot days and long walks, offer small drinks every 15–20 minutes rather than letting your dog gulp a huge amount at the end.
  • Encourage reluctant drinkers with a clean bowl changed daily, a pet fountain, or a splash of water mixed into kibble.

This calculator is an educational estimate, not veterinary advice. Ask your vet about your dog's specific needs, especially for puppies, seniors and dogs with a medical condition.
