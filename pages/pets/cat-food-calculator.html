How this cat food calculator works

Vets estimate a cat's calorie needs the same way they do for dogs, but with lower multipliers. First comes the resting energy requirement (RER), the calories a cat burns at rest. That is multiplied by a life stage factor to get the daily energy requirement. These factors follow the WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee toolkit and the AAHA nutrition and weight management guidelines.

Once you have daily calories, the portions are simple division by the calorie counts on your food labels. That matters more for cats than for most pets: a small can can hold anywhere from 70 to over 100 kcal, and dry cat foods range from roughly 300 to over 500 kcal per cup. If you feed both, pick a split and the calculator divides the calories between them.

The formula

weight in kg = weight in lb × 0.4536
RER = 70 × (weight in kg)0.75
daily calories = RER × life stage factor
cans per day = (daily calories × wet share) ÷ kcal per can
cups per day = (daily calories × dry share) ÷ kcal per cup

In practice: about one and a half small cans plus a level third of a cup of kibble, spread over two or more meals.

Tips for feeding the right amount

  • Weigh dry food if you can. A third of a cup of kibble is only about 30–40 grams, so small scooping errors add up fast. A kitchen scale is far more accurate than a cup.
  • Check body condition. You should be able to feel your cat's ribs with light pressure and see a slight waist from above. Adjust portions by 10% and recheck in 3–4 weeks.
  • Most indoor cats need less than the bag says. Feeding guides are written broadly; neutered, indoor cats often need 10–20% less.
  • Use the ideal weight for weight loss. Calculating from an overweight cat's current weight will keep it overweight.
  • Leftover wet food: cover and refrigerate opened cans and use within about 2–3 days; warm to room temperature before serving.

This calculator is an educational estimate, not veterinary advice. Check your food labels and ask your vet, especially for kittens, pregnant or nursing cats, seniors, or cats with a medical condition.
