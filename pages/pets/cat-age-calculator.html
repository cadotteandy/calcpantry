How cat years convert to human years

The old "multiply by seven" rule doesn't fit cats at all. Cats grow up fast: by their first birthday they're roughly as developed as a 15-year-old person, and by two they're comparable to a 24-year-old adult. After that, aging slows to a steady pace of about 4 human years for every cat year.

This calculator uses the age-equivalence chart published with the 2021 AAHA/AAFP Feline Life Stage Guidelines, the reference most vets use. For kittens under two, it interpolates between the chart's points (about 1 human year at 1 month, 4 at 3 months, 10 at 6 months, 15 at 12 months and 21 at 18 months).

The formula

age 1 year = 15 human years
age 2 years = 24 human years
over 2: human age = 24 + 4 × (cat age in years − 2)

The four feline life stages

  • Kitten (birth to 1 year): rapid growth, vaccinations, spay or neuter, and learning to accept handling and carriers.
  • Young adult (1 through 6 years): the time to set healthy habits: a lean body weight, dental care and yearly exams.
  • Mature adult (7 through 10 years): weight gain and dental disease become more common; some vets start routine bloodwork.
  • Senior (over 10 years): watch for kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, arthritis and weight loss. Twice-yearly checkups are recommended.

The guidelines also describe an end-of-life stage, which can happen at any age and focuses on comfort and quality of life. Where exactly a cat turns "senior" is a guideline, not a switch: the source says "over 10 years," so this calculator starts the senior stage just after the 10th birthday, and your vet may treat a fit 11-year-old like a mature adult.

Keep in mind

Human-year equivalents are a comparison tool, not a biological measurement. Genetics, diet, weight and indoor versus outdoor life all change how a particular cat ages. Changes in appetite, thirst, litter box habits or weight are worth a vet visit at any age.
