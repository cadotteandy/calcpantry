How dog years convert to human years

The "multiply by seven" rule is a myth. Dogs grow up very fast: a one-year-old dog is roughly as developed as a 15-year-old person, and a two-year-old is comparable to a young adult in their mid-20s. After that, aging slows, but how much it slows depends on size. Large and giant breeds age faster and have shorter lifespans than small dogs.

The default method here is the rule of thumb published by the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA): the first year equals about 15 human years, the second adds about 9, and each year after that adds roughly 4 to 7 human years depending on size. Size-based charts from AKC and many vet clinics follow the same pattern, with small differences in the exact numbers, so expect other calculators to be a year or two apart.

The formulas

Size-based chart:
age 1 = 15 human years, age 2 = 24 human years
over 2: human age = 24 + (dog age − 2) × Y
Y = 4 small, 5 medium, 6 large, 7 giant

Epigenetic (UCSD) formula, age 1 and up:
human age = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31

The epigenetic formula

In a study published in Cell Systems (preprint 2019, journal 2020), researchers at UC San Diego tracked age-related chemical marks on DNA, called methylation, in 104 Labrador retrievers and compared them with human data. The best fit was a logarithm: dogs race through the equivalent of human childhood and early adulthood in a couple of years, then the curve flattens. "ln" is the natural logarithm. The formula only makes sense from age 1; below that it drops toward zero and then goes negative. Because it came from a single breed, it doesn't account for size, which is why this calculator uses the size-based chart by default and shows the epigenetic answer for comparison.

Life stages

The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines group dogs into puppy, young adult, mature adult and senior, with senior defined as the last 25% of the expected lifespan rather than a fixed birthday. This calculator uses these approximate cut-offs:

  • Puppy: under 1 year (under 18 months for giant breeds, which keep growing longer).
  • Young adult: until about 3 years.
  • Mature adult: from 3 years until the senior stage.
  • Senior: from about 10 years (small), 9 (medium), 8 (large) or 6 (giant).

Keep in mind

Human-year equivalents are a way to picture your dog's age, not a biological test. Genetics, body weight, exercise and dental health all affect how an individual dog ages; keeping a dog lean is one of the best-documented ways to add healthy years. Slowing down, stiffness, weight changes or increased thirst are worth a vet visit at any age.
