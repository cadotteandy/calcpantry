How this puppy weight calculator works

Puppies don't grow at a steady rate. They pile on weight fastest in the first few months, then slow down as they approach adult size, and the timing depends heavily on breed size. A Chihuahua is close to full grown at 9 months, while a Great Dane can keep growing until two years old. So the useful question is: what share of its adult weight does a puppy of this size group usually carry at this age?

This calculator uses a typical growth curve for each size group, built from the rules of thumb vets and breeders have used for decades:

  • Toy / small: weight at 6 weeks × 4 (about 25%), or weight at 8 weeks × 3 (about 33%).
  • Medium: weight at 14 weeks × 2.5 (about 40%).
  • Large: roughly half of adult weight at about 5 months (20–22 weeks).
  • Giant: roughly half of adult weight at 6–7 months, with growth continuing to 18–24 months.

Between those anchor points, the curve is smoothed so you can enter any age. The result is shown as a range of ±15% around the best estimate, because that is realistically how precise this kind of prediction is.

The formula

share now = typical % of adult weight for this age and size
estimated adult weight = current weight ÷ share now
range = estimate × 0.85 to estimate × 1.15

Why not "weight ÷ weeks × 52"?

That popular formula assumes a puppy gains the same amount every week for a year. For the example puppy it predicts 52 lb, far above what a medium dog at 35% of adult weight will reach, because growth slows sharply after the first few months. It can be a reasonable check for small breeds around 4 to 6 months, but it overestimates young puppies.

Tips for a better estimate

  • Look at the parents. For a purebred, the adult weight of the mother and father beats any formula. Mixed breeds most often land between the two parents.
  • Weigh regularly on the same scale. A weekly or biweekly weight log is the best way to spot a puppy tracking above or below its curve.
  • Enter an older age when you can. A 16-week estimate is more reliable than an 8-week one.
  • Don't push growth. Large and giant puppies should grow steadily, not fast. Feed a food made for large-breed puppies and keep them lean, which is linked to healthier joints.
  • Judge body condition too. A heavy puppy is not necessarily a big-framed one; fat puppies inflate the estimate.

This calculator gives a rough estimate for planning, not a prediction you can count on, and it is not veterinary advice. Your vet can track your puppy's growth and body condition at each visit.
