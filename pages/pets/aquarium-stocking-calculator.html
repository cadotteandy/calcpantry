How this stocking calculator works

The starting point is the old hobby rule of thumb: one inch of adult fish per US gallon of water. It's crude, but for small, slim community fish it gives a sensible first answer. On its own it has two big flaws, so this calculator makes two adjustments.

1. Body bulk. Waste output tracks body mass, not length. A 6-inch angelfish weighs many times more than four 1.5-inch neon tetras. Each species gets a bioload factor: 1.0 for slim fish, 1.25 for deeper-bodied fish like platies and gouramis, 1.5 for mollies and bristlenose plecos, 2.0 for angelfish, and 0.5 for the thin, eel-like kuhli loach.

2. Filtration. A filter rated for more water than your tank processes waste with some headroom. A filter rated at 1.5× your tank adds 10% capacity, 2× or more adds the maximum 20%. An undersized filter reduces capacity (down to −30%).

The formula

stocking length = Σ (adult length × bioload factor × quantity)
filter ratio = filter rating ÷ tank volume
capacity = tank gallons × (1 + 0.2 × (ratio − 1)), capped between 0.7× and 1.2×
% stocked = stocking length ÷ capacity

Under 60% is understocked (room to add), 60–100% is good, 100–130% is heavily stocked (workable with strong filtration and weekly water changes), and over 130% is overstocked.

What the rule can't tell you

  • Swimming space and shape. Danios need length to swim; angelfish need height. Gallons alone don't capture that.
  • Temperament. A tank can be "good" on paper and still fail if fish fight. Research compatibility.
  • Your maintenance. Weekly 25–30% water changes support more fish than monthly ones. Test nitrate to see how you're really doing.
  • Real water volume. Gravel and decor displace water; a "20 gallon" tank often holds 16–18.

This is a planning guide, not a guarantee of fish health. Cycle your tank before adding fish, stock gradually, and ask a knowledgeable fish store or aquatic vet about specific species.
