How cat due dates are calculated

A cat's pregnancy lasts about 63 to 65 days from mating, a little over nine weeks. Normal pregnancies range from roughly 61 to 72 days, so this calculator gives a single estimated due date at day 63, a most-likely window of days 63–65 and the full normal range.

The range exists because cats are induced ovulators. A queen doesn't release eggs on a fixed schedule; mating triggers ovulation about 24 to 36 hours later, and it often takes several matings to trigger it. A cat in heat can mate repeatedly over four to seven days, sometimes with more than one tom, so the exact day of conception is rarely known. That is why you should enter the first mating date.

The formula

due date = mating date + 63 days
most likely window = mating date + 63 to 65 days
normal range = mating date + 61 to 72 days
days along = today − mating date

Signs and stages week by week

Weeks 1–3 (days 0–21)

Fertilized eggs reach the uterus and implant around days 12–14. Her heat behavior stops, and some cats sleep more or eat a little less. The first visible sign is usually pinking around days 15–18, when the nipples swell and turn a deeper pink. It is easiest to spot in a first pregnancy.

Weeks 4–6 (days 22–42)

This is the best time to confirm the pregnancy. A vet can often feel the walnut-sized swellings of developing kittens by palpation up to about day 25; after that the uterus fills out and they are harder to feel individually. Ultrasound shows heartbeats from about three weeks on. Her appetite picks up and her belly starts to round.

Weeks 7–9 (days 43–63+)

From about day 45 kitten skeletons are visible on an x-ray, and a film taken after day 55 gives the most reliable count, which helps you know when she has finished delivering. In the last week she will look for a quiet, enclosed place to nest. Labor usually follows within a day of restlessness, loss of appetite and heavy grooming.

Practical tips

  • Switch to kitten food. Pregnant and nursing queens need more energy, protein and calcium; a complete kitten or all-life-stages food provides it. Let her eat more as pregnancy progresses, and skip supplements unless your vet prescribes them.
  • Prepare a nesting box early. A cardboard box lined with towels in a warm, quiet room, set up a week or two before the due date, gives her time to accept it.
  • Keep her indoors. Late-pregnancy cats may wander off to give birth somewhere hidden.
  • Know when to call. Twenty to thirty minutes of hard straining with no kitten, more than two hours between kittens, or a foul discharge all need a vet.
  • Plan the next step. Cats can come back into heat within weeks of giving birth, even while nursing. Ask your vet about spaying once the kittens are weaned.

This calculator gives estimates for planning and is not veterinary advice. Have your vet confirm the pregnancy and supervise her care.
