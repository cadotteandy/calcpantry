What goes into the cost of a pet

Most of what you spend on a dog or cat is predictable once you list it out: food, routine vet care, parasite prevention, grooming, toys and treats, and care while you travel. On top of that sit optional but increasingly common costs such as pet insurance, plus a bump in the first year for adoption, spay or neuter surgery and start-up supplies.

Published surveys give wide ranges. Rover's 2025 cost report put dogs at about $1,390 to $5,295 a year and cats at about $830 to $3,095, with shelter adoption fees of roughly $30 to $455 for dogs. The low end is a healthy pet with no insurance, no boarding and home grooming; the high end includes premium food, insurance, regular grooming and frequent pet sitting. This calculator's presets sit in the middle and are meant to be edited.

The formula

yearly cost = food + vet + vaccines + prevention + insurance + grooming + toys & treats + boarding + license + supplies
monthly cost = yearly cost ÷ 12
first-year total = yearly cost + adoption + spay/neuter + start-up supplies
lifetime estimate = first-year total + yearly cost × (lifespan − 1)

Where the preset numbers come from

  • Insurance: NAPHIA's 2025 State of the Industry report lists average accident and illness premiums of about $749 a year for dogs and $386 for cats in 2024. Presets scale the dog figure by size, because larger breeds usually cost more to insure.
  • Overall ranges and adoption fees: Rover's 2025 True Cost of Pet Parenthood survey of 1,000 US pet parents.
  • Food: scales with calories. A giant dog can eat four times what a small dog does; use the dog food calculator to price your own brand.
  • Lifespan: small dogs commonly live 13–16 years, medium dogs 10–13, large dogs 10–12 and giant breeds 8–10; indoor cats often reach 13–17.

Tips for budgeting

  • Price your own area. Call a local vet for exam and vaccine prices; urban clinics can charge twice as much as rural ones.
  • Check what the adoption fee includes. Many shelters include spay or neuter, first vaccines and a microchip, so set those lines to zero if they're covered.
  • Plan for age. Senior pets often need dental cleanings ($300–$1,000+), bloodwork and daily medication, so budget more for the last third of their life.
  • Build an emergency cushion. Whether or not you insure, keep $1,000–$2,000 set aside for deductibles and surprises.

Costs are typical US estimates for planning, not quotes. Prices vary widely by region, breed and health.
