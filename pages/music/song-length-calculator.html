How song length is worked out

Tempo tells you how many beats pass each minute, and the time signature tells you how many beats are in a bar. Multiply bars by beats per bar to get the total beats, divide by the BPM, and you have the length in minutes. It's handy for planning arrangements, cutting a track to fit a video or ad slot, or checking how long an intro really is.

The one thing to watch is what your BPM counts. In 4/4 and 3/4 it's always the quarter note. In meters like 6/8 it varies: most DAWs keep counting quarter notes by default (a 6/8 bar is three quarter notes long), musicians often feel two dotted-quarter beats per bar, and some charts count every eighth note. Pick the option that matches your tempo.

The formula

bar length in quarter notes = top × 4 ÷ bottom
beats per bar = bar length ÷ length of the counted beat (quarter = 1, dotted quarter = 1.5, eighth = 0.5)
seconds per bar = beats per bar × 60 ÷ BPM
length (s) = bars × seconds per bar   ·   bars = length (s) ÷ seconds per bar

Tips for arranging to a length

  • Think in 4- and 8-bar phrases. If you need 105 bars, an extra bar of silence or a 4-bar tag usually sounds better than an odd phrase.
  • Leave room for the tail. Reverb and delay ring past the last bar; allow a second or two before a hard cut.
  • Fit to picture by nudging tempo. Small tempo changes of 1 to 2 BPM rarely feel different but can shift the end by several seconds.
  • Check DAW settings for compound meters; if the arrangement runs long or short by a factor of 1.5 or 2, the BPM is counting a different note.
