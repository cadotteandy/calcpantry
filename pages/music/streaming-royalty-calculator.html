How streaming royalties really work

No streaming service pays a set amount per play. Most use a pro-rata model: each month, in each country, they put a share of their subscription and ad revenue into a pool and divide it among rights holders according to their share of all streams. Your effective per-stream rate therefore moves with where your fans live, how many are paying subscribers, the exchange rate and your contract. The money flows to the owner of the recording, usually via a distributor or label, who then pays you.

That's why this calculator uses rough averages drawn from the per-stream figures widely reported in 2024–2025 industry analyses. Treat the results as a ballpark, and replace the rates with numbers from your own distributor statements once you have them.

The formula

platform payout = streams × average $ per stream
gross = sum of all platforms
after distributor = gross × (1 − distributor %)
your share = after distributor × your %

Tips

  • Check the Spotify threshold. Since 2024, a track needs at least 1,000 streams in the past 12 months to earn recorded royalties on Spotify.
  • Register your songs too. If you write your own music, sign up with a performing rights organization and a publishing administrator to collect songwriting royalties, which this calculator doesn't include.
  • Compare distributor models. Some charge a yearly fee and keep 0% of royalties; others charge nothing upfront but take a percentage. The better deal depends on how much you earn.
  • Agree on splits in writing before release, including who owns the master and how producers and featured artists are paid.
