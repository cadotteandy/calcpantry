Why sync delay and reverb to the tempo

A delay set to a note value of the song repeats in rhythm, so the echoes lock into the groove instead of blurring it. The same goes for reverb pre-delay and the rate of tremolo, auto-pan or filter LFOs. Most plugins have a sync button, but hardware pedals, older units and some reverbs still want a number in milliseconds or hertz. That's what this table gives you.

The formula

quarter note (ms) = 60,000 ÷ BPM
note (ms) = quarter note × length in quarter notes (whole = 4, eighth = 0.5 …)
dotted = note × 1.5   ·   triplet = note × 2/3
rate (Hz) = 1,000 ÷ note (ms)
BPM from a delay = 60,000 × length in quarter notes ÷ delay (ms)

There are 60,000 milliseconds in a minute, and BPM counts quarter-note beats per minute, so dividing one by the other gives the length of one beat.

Example: a song at 120 BPM

At 120 BPM a quarter note is 60,000 ÷ 120 = 500 ms, a dotted eighth is 375 ms and one bar of 4/4 is 2,000 ms. Here is the full set of note lengths in milliseconds:

Tips for tempo-synced effects

  • Dotted eighth for guitar. Play straight eighths against a dotted-eighth delay and the repeats fill the gaps between your notes.
  • Quarter or eighth for vocals. A quarter-note slapback-style repeat, mixed low and filtered, adds depth without cluttering the lyric.
  • Short pre-delay keeps vocals clear. A 1/32 or 1/64 note of pre-delay lets the dry word speak before the reverb arrives.
  • Round to taste. A millisecond or two either way is inaudible; set the time slightly shorter if the repeats feel like they drag.
