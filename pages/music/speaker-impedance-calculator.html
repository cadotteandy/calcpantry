How speaker wiring changes impedance

Impedance is the load a speaker presents to the amplifier, measured in ohms (Ω). Wiring speakers together changes the total load. Series wiring (positive of one speaker to negative of the next) adds the impedances. Parallel wiring (all positives together, all negatives together) lowers it. Series-parallel combines both to keep the total near one speaker's rating while driving more speakers.

The amp's minimum load is the lowest impedance it's designed to drive, often 4 or 8 Ω for home and guitar amps, and sometimes 2 Ω for car amps. Go below it and the amp works harder than it should.

The formula

series: Z = Z1 + Z2 + … (identical: Z × N)
parallel: 1 ÷ Z = 1 ÷ Z1 + 1 ÷ Z2 + … (identical: Z ÷ N)
series-parallel pairs: Z = 2 × Zspeaker ÷ (N ÷ 2)
power to speaker i in parallel = P × (1 ÷ Zi) ÷ Σ(1 ÷ Z)

Tips

  • Never go below the minimum. Equal to it is fine; higher is safe but quieter on solid-state amps.
  • Match the tap on tube amps. Set the output selector to the total impedance and never run a tube amp without a load.
  • Mind the power ratings. Each speaker should handle at least its share of the amp's power, with some headroom.
  • Keep polarity consistent. Reversing one speaker's wires makes it cancel the others and thins the bass.
